## M1 Sim core
- **EHAM data is generated by `scripts/build-eham.py`** from two downloaded sources, then committed as `data/airports/EHAM.json`:
  - Runway thresholds, fix coordinates and the TMA polygon: openScope `assets/airports/eham.json` (MIT; itself sourced from the Dutch eAIP, AIRAC 2016-07-07). https://github.com/openscope/openscope
  - Runway length and width: OurAirports `runways.csv` (public domain). https://ourairports.com/data/
  - Hand-set in the script: ILS 18R (3° glideslope, 50 ft TCH, 25 NM range), frequencies, transition-level table, hold inbound tracks, departure exits, airline/type mix.
  - Not cross-checked against the current eAIP in this session; fix positions are 2016 data.
- **Headings are true, magnetic variation ignored** (real EHAM variation is about 2°E) — keeps runway numbers, headings and the scope consistent without a conversion layer.
- **Flat-earth projection** (equirectangular around the airport reference point), positions in NM — error is negligible inside 60 NM.
- **Wind is one surface wind applied at all altitudes** (spec §5: "data-driven surface wind").
- **TAS = IAS × (1 + 2 % per 1000 ft)**; turn rate = standard rate 3°/s limited by a 25° bank.
- **Performance table is simplified** (`src/core/aircraft/perf.ts`): one climb rate, one descent rate and a typical approach speed per type. Pilots refuse speeds below Vapp or above the type's maximum.
- **Simplified STARs:** arrivals spawn 12 NM outside their IAF on the bearing of a real preceding STAR fix, level at FL070–FL100, 250 kt, direct to the IAF. After the IAF with no instruction they keep their heading (real procedure would be to hold; kept simple so beginners see obvious consequences).
- **Holds:** right-hand, 1-minute outbound leg, direct entry only (no parallel/teardrop entries). Inbound tracks ARTIP 253°, RIVER 046°, SUGOL 112° approximate the published holds.
- **Transition level table:** QNH ≥ 1013 → FL040, 977–1012 → FL045, below 977 → FL050 (simplified; spec §5).
- **Departures from 24 use only exits LEKKO, LOPIK, ARNEM, VALKO** so they never cross the 18R final; they fly runway track to 600 ft, then direct to the exit, climbing to FL060. Handoff target is "Amsterdam Radar 125.750".
- **Tower handoff is refused until the aircraft is on the localizer** ("unable, not yet established"). Unrealistic (a real pilot would just switch) but stops one misheard voice command from losing an aircraft.
- **ILS:** localizer capture uses a turn-anticipation lead distance. Pro: intercept ≤ 30°, glideslope only from at or below, stable gate at 4 NM (on LOC, on GS, ≤ 200 kt) else go-around. Beginner: any intercept angle below 150°, dives up to 3500 fpm to catch the glideslope, go-around only if still more than 300 ft high at 2 NM.
- **Go-around:** runway heading, climb to 2000 ft (EHAM 18R missed approach initial altitude), 200 kt, back on Approach frequency.
- **Speed schedule without an assigned speed:** 250 kt at/above 6000 ft, 220 below, 210 on the localizer, 180 inside 12 NM, Vapp inside 5 NM (or inside the "until N miles" distance).
- **Wake minima:** ICAO Doc 4444 §8.7.3.4 distance-based table (J→H 6, J→M 7, J→L 8, H→H 4, H→M 5, H→L 6, M→L 5 NM), applied to pairs that are both on the localizer.
- **Conflict prediction:** straight-line extrapolation of track and groundspeed, 120 s look-ahead, 5 s steps; altitude moves toward the cleared level at the type's rate.
- **Leaving the airspace:** an aircraft that has been inside the TMA polygon and is now outside it is removed, unless it is holding or flying direct to a fix (the IAF holds straddle the boundary).
- **Light wake category:** added Jetfly (JFA, "Mosquito") with the PC-12 so wake rules have a Light type to work with; it is not in the spec's callsign table.

## M2–M7 (scope, phraseology, radio, voice, game layer, realistic display)
- **Parser is a hand-written token matcher** (no grammar library): words → number-merged tokens → ordered matchers. Corpus (`tests/phrases/*.jsonl`, 651 cases) is generated by `scripts/gen-corpus.py` with expected results derived from the templates, not from the parser; `manual.jsonl` is hand-written. The corpus is kept 100 % green rather than at the spec's 95 % floor so regressions show up.
- **Callsign score weights the flight number 65 % and the airline 35 %**: "Speedbird 111" must not match "Speedbird 432". Accept threshold 0.72; two candidates within 0.08 → ambiguous → "station calling, say again".
- **Typed text may omit the callsign** (it goes to the selected aircraft), unless the text starts with an airline name/code: an explicit unknown callsign never falls back to the selection.
- **"to" before a number:** "heading to seven zero" is read as 270 (common ASR error); "descend to thousand" as 2000. Otherwise "to" is ignored and flagged (`to-before-number`).
- **QNH check:** the first clearance to an altitude at or below 3000 ft must carry the QNH (`qnh-missing`); later ones need not.
- **Feedback rules are non-blocking:** every understood instruction is flown; issues only cost phraseology score (warn −20, info −5, per transmission, floor 0).
- **Pilot reads back only on the air:** the instruction takes effect when the readback starts, as in reality. Readbacks are generated from the commands the pilot *understood*, so a Pro readback error also changes what is flown.
- **Readback errors (Pro):** one numeric element (heading ±10/20°, level ±1000 ft, speed ±10/20 kt) per affected readback. Caught if the player says "negative"/"correction" or repeats the correct value within 45 s of sim time; otherwise marked missed and only revealed in the debrief.
- **Frequency blocking (Pro):** keying the mic while a pilot transmits cuts the pilot off, marks both as blocked, and the pilot retries 2.5 s later.
- **Beginner auto-handoffs:** arrivals to Tower inside 8 NM once established; departures to Radar above 5500 ft.
- **Pro pilot requests:** an arrival kept at or above 6000 ft within 18 NM may ask "request descent" (once). "Unable" replies come from command validation (speed limits, runway not in use, etc.).
- **Scoring weights** (`src/game/scoring.ts`): Beginner only rewards landings/departures/phraseology and lightly penalises go-arounds and lost aircraft; Pro adds −200 per separation loss, −100 per wake infringement, −50 per missed handoff/go-around, −150 per aircraft lost, −75 per uncaught readback error, +25 per caught one, −1 per 10 s delay.
- **TTS: Piper via ONNX Runtime Web + the `@diffusionstudio/piper-wasm` espeak-ng phonemizer, wrapped in our own worker** (`src/speech/tts.worker.ts`). Evaluated `@mintplex-labs/piper-tts-web` (most recently maintained port, MIT): it hard-codes speaker 0 and loads runtimes from CDNs, so it can't do per-aircraft voices from one multi-speaker model. Our worker is ~100 lines using the same pieces.
- **One Piper model, many voices:** `en_GB-vctk-medium` (109 speakers, ~77 MB, CC BY 4.0) gives distinct accents from one download. 8 speakers picked (English, Scottish, Irish, American, Indian, Australian; male and female), assigned by hashing the callsign.
- **Fallbacks:** while Piper downloads or if it fails → `speechSynthesis`; if that is missing → silent but correctly timed transmissions.
- **ONNX Runtime and phonemizer WASM are self-hosted** (copied to `public/vendor/` at build time); only model weights come from Hugging Face. onnxruntime-web pinned to the same build transformers.js uses so one runtime serves TTS and ASR.
- **No WASM threads:** GitHub Pages can't send cross-origin-isolation headers, so `numThreads = 1`.
- **ASR: Whisper base.en on WebGPU (fp32 encoder + q4 decoder, ~206 MB) → Whisper tiny.en q8 on WASM (~41 MB)** if WebGPU is missing or base fails to load → Web Speech API only if picked in settings (labelled "may use a cloud service"). Models: `Xenova/whisper-*.en` (Apache-2.0). Live transcript = re-transcribing the buffer about once a second while PTT is held, only if a timed warm-up transcription takes under 500 ms (on slow WebGPU, e.g. Firefox on Linux, the partials queued up and delayed the final transcript to 5–8 s); recordings under 0.35 s or near-silent are dropped (Whisper invents text for silence).
- **Model caching:** Piper weights go into the Cache API (`vectors-models`); transformers.js uses its own browser cache.
- **Realistic display:** positions update on a 4 s sweep with 5 history dots; data blocks show callsign / level + groundspeed in tens; position symbol is the owning unit's letter (A/T/D); unassociated targets are green crosses. No conflict prediction or intent lines in Pro on this display (spec §8). DCB buttons implemented: range, place centre, range rings, maps, brightness, leader direction, character size, pause, rate, display switch, preferences, end.
- **Strips:** drag to reorder, or Alt+Up/Down with the keyboard (accessibility); unplaced aircraft sort by ETA.
- **Tutorial:** 5 lessons, each step completes from sim state (not from parsing), so voice and typing both work. Lesson 5 runs Pro rules so the handoff is manual.
- **Pro scenarios:** light (18 arr/h, 20 min), medium (26 arr/h, heavies, 25 min), peak (36 arr/h, 22 kt crosswind, QNH 998 → TL FL045, 30 min). Seeds fixed so runs are comparable.
- **Live partial transcripts only on WebGPU.** On the single-threaded WASM path, re-transcribing every second queued behind the final transcription (6–8 s latency); without partials the final arrives in ~2 s.
- **Lessons never inject readback errors**, even when the lesson uses Pro rules.
- **Free play traffic rate is a setting** (2–40 arrivals/h, default 12), spec §8 "low, adjustable".
- **Dev-only hooks** `window.__vectors` / `window.__drawMs` exist only under `import.meta.env.DEV` (stripped from production builds).

## M8 Polish
- **Pilot clips normalised to 0.9 peak** in the TTS worker (raw Piper output peaked at ~0.17 and varied by speaker); the radio effect's pre-gain went from 1.6 to 1 to match.
- **Accessibility checked with axe-core 4.10** (headless Chromium) on every screen. Canvas text isn't covered by axe, so scope label colours were checked by hand against WCAG 4.5:1 (ring labels, fix names, untracked targets: #7D8BA3 on the scope background ≈ 5.2:1; Realistic fix labels #828282 on black ≈ 5.5:1).
- **Game screen has a visually hidden `<h1>`** inside the scope's `<main>` landmark, for screen-reader navigation.
- **Dialogs are modal for the keyboard** (focus moved in, Tab wrapped, Escape closes Settings, focus restored); the global PTT key is ignored while focus is inside a dialog so Space can toggle checkboxes.
- **Learn reference screen** (menu, top bar, Realistic DCB "LEARN"): numbers and phonetic alphabet (ICAO Annex 10 Vol II §5.2.1.4 pronunciation), saying values and callsigns, every command with its canonical phrase and readback, levels and altimetry, approach concepts. Example phrases are generated by the same phrase code the pilots use (`controllerPhrase`, `readback`, `spell`, `spokenAltitude`), so the reference can't drift from the game. The transition-level table, wake minima, frequencies and IAFs are read from EHAM.json and the core. Opening it in a game pauses the sim and restores the previous pause state on close. ▶ buttons speak the example in a pilot voice (silent when voices are off).
- **Spoken phonetic letters** (Learn screen): Piper garbles a single isolated word: the first sound is lost ("Mike" → "bye", "Papa" → "ha", "Zulu" → "do"). Checked by synthesising every letter in Node with the same model and transcribing it with Whisper small.en as a stand-in listener. Bare words were recognised about 30 % of the time; "M, as in Mike." on VCTK speaker p225 was recognised 71/78 times (3 takes × 26 letters), the best of the phrasings and speakers tried. Learn letters use that phrasing and that voice. Slower speech (length scale 1.2–1.4) and leading silence phonemes didn't help.
- **Quebec is respelled "Kebeck"** before phonemizing: espeak says "kwi-BECK", ICAO Annex 10 gives "keh-BECK". Audio only; displayed text keeps "Quebec".

## Tower mode (docs/design/tower3d.md)
- **Scope B:** tower controller (landing, line-up, take-off, vacate, handoffs), with an AI Approach. No ground control or taxi yet.
- **Two cabs, as at the real Schiphol:** Tower West for the Polderbaan (18R) and the central tower for 24. The Polderbaan is too far from the central tower to see, which is why Schiphol has a separate tower there ([schiphol.nl](https://www.schiphol.nl/en/blog/the-schiphol-tower)). In the game, one player works both cabs.
- **Tower positions from OpenStreetMap** (`aeroway=tower`): Centre 52.30752 N 4.76244 E, height 101.17 m; West 52.3317 N 4.71787 E, height 60 m. Eye heights (92 m and 54 m) are assumptions.
- **Mockups are renders of a real three.js scene** (`docs/design/tower3d-sketch/`), not drawings, so the design can't promise a look the renderer won't produce. Aircraft are procedural low-poly with real dimensions: no model files, no licences.
- **Art direction changed to clean semi-realistic** after a style test: aerial-photo ground, gradient sky, smooth procedural aircraft. The low-poly boards are superseded.
- *(Superseded 2026-11-16: photos removed.)* **Ground imagery: PDOK Luchtfoto 2024, CC BY 4.0** (licence checked on data.overheid.nl for the dataset behind `service.pdok.nl/hwh/luchtfotorgb/wms/v1_0`). Fetched in EPSG:4326, so each tile is linear in lat/lon, the same as the core's flat-earth projection: the photo and the sim agree exactly. Tiers: base 30 m/px, airport 4 m/px, 18R and 24 corridors 1–1.2 m/px; WebP, ≤2048 px per tile, 5.2 MB in total (budget 8 MB). Colours boosted (saturation ×1.45, contrast ×1.08) because raw orthophotos look grey under the haze.
- **Tiers drawn without depth testing, stacked by render order** (finer tiers on top). Avoids z-fighting between coplanar tiles without a logarithmic depth buffer.
- *(Fixed in T2: `build-eham.py` moves it 70 m along the runway.)* **Runway 24's data point is ~70 m north-east of the painted threshold** on the photo (the 18R one is on the threshold bars). The data comes from openScope, which seems to give the runway end. The drawn markings follow the sim data, so landings match them; the drawn 24 threshold sits ~45 m past the OSM pavement end. Fix the data together with runway exits in T2.
- **Tower West default view 352°**: the 18R touchdown zone is at 352.6° and the final at 356–360°, so both fit in the 28° horizontal field of view.
- **One sim clock for all views:** `Session.frame(timestamp)` advances only once per animation frame, whichever view calls it.
- **Tower mode is a scenario property** (`view: 'tower'`), not a display setting: tower scenarios will have their own traffic and rules (T4). The Realistic display setting is ignored there for now.
- **Pitch and gear are derived in the renderer** (`aircraftPose`), not modelled in the core: pitch = flight-path angle + a typical angle of attack; gear down on the ground, below 2000 ft AGL for arrivals and 300 ft for departures. The core gains real `gearDown`/`pitch` in T2 if the rules need them.
- **Shadows are a soft decal** displaced away from the sun by height, not a shadow map: cheap, and it works for every aircraft at any distance.
- **Preview shortcut:** in the tower preview, arrivals are cleared for the ILS automatically when they spawn. The AI Approach replaces this in T4.
- **Airport surfaces drawn from OpenStreetMap, not photographed** (the user found the photo-only ground poor). Runway areas, taxiway centrelines with `width` (default 23 m), aprons (multipolygons stitched), runway holding positions, and `landuse=grass` inside the aerodrome boundary, simplified to 1 m. Built by `scripts/build-tower-ground.py` into `public/tower/ground.json`, which stays under the ODbL (derived database); credited on screen.
- **Runway markings follow ICAO Annex 14 vol. I §5.2, simplified:** threshold stripes 30 m × 1.8 m from 6 m past the threshold (16 on a 60 m runway, 12 on 45 m); designator 9 m tall, letter nearer the threshold; aiming point 60 × 10 m from 400 m; touchdown zone pairs every 150 m in a 3-3-2-2-1-1 pattern (skipping the aiming point); centreline 30 m stripes with 20 m gaps, 0.9 m wide; 0.9 m edge stripes. Holding positions are pattern A with 0.4 m lines (real: 0.15 m), so they stay visible from the cab.
- **A runway is drawn from threshold to threshold**, not with `lengthFt`: that figure (OurAirports) is the whole pavement, 100–270 m longer than the distance between thresholds at EHAM. Pavement beyond the thresholds comes from the OSM runway areas.
- **Photos dropped; the surroundings are modelled from OSM** (2026-11-16, user's choice after a side-by-side sketch). Land use, water and motorways (ODbL) in `public/tower/land.json`; airport buildings extruded. 1.6 MB compressed against 4.7 MB of photos. Fields get a seeded shade each from a short palette so the polder reads as a patchwork.
- **Building heights:** OSM `height`, else `building:levels` × 3.5 m (+2 m per roof level), else a default per kind (hangar 22 m, terminal 18 m, office 18 m, industrial 10 m, other 9 m). Assumed values, not surveyed.
- **Land detail limited to about 10 km** around EHAM (water and motorways out to about 15 km): from a 54–92 m eye anything further is a sliver in the haze, and one large Overpass request was truncated at 55 MB.
- **Runway exits from OSM** (T2): taxiways with a `ref` whose end lies on the runway centreline, cut at the holding position 95 m out (Annex 14 table 3-2 gives 90 m for a code 4 precision runway; OSM holds are a few metres further out). 18R: V1–V4 on the left, at 1643, 2236, 2837 and 3478 m. Names not checked against the eAIP aerodrome chart; a Staatscourant notice does name taxiway V along the Polderbaan ([stcrt 2018-15708](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2018-15708.pdf)).
- **Departures enter 24 at S7E**, the entry nearest its start (60 m past the threshold). Simplification: every departure uses it.
- **Ground handling, simplified:** braking 3 kt/s (about 1.5 m/s², a low autobrake setting); a rapid exit (turn of 45° or less) is taken at 35 kt, other exits at 15 kt; along the exit 25 kt, taxi 15 kt, turn rate 20°/s (about 22 m radius); take-off acceleration 4 kt/s. A pilot picks the first exit reachable at normal braking, with 30 m margin. Typical result: touchdown about 330 m in, V2 for every type, off the runway about 50 s after touchdown.
- **Touchdown where the 3° glide path meets the runway** (about 290 m past the threshold with a 50 ft TCH), not at the threshold. No flare manoeuvre is modelled.
- **Runway occupancy:** from line-up (crossing the holding position) or touchdown until the aircraft reaches the holding position on its exit, and for departures until airborne past the far end (or above 1500 ft). Both directions of a runway count as the same runway. A landing is scored at touchdown (`landed` event; about 20 s earlier than before T2, when it fired at 40 kt on the rollout), and the aircraft is removed when it reaches the holding position (`vacated`; no ground movement to the gates: ground control is a non-goal).
- **Stand-in tower until T3:** departures hold at least 20 s, line up when the runway is free and no arrival is within 2 NM of it, wait 8 s lined up, then roll. Approach mode keeps this permanently (the player there isn't Tower); tower scenarios will switch it off. No departure wake spacing yet (T4).
- **Ground traffic is hidden on the approach scopes** (`onRadar`): departures at the holding point or lining up, and arrivals on their exit, are not drawn; take-off rolls and rollouts are, as before T2. Approach radar doesn't show ground movements, and they cluttered the airport.
- **Ground and Departure frequencies from the eAIP** (2026-11-17, checked by the user; earlier placeholders 121.800 / 120.555): Ground 121.560 is the one for runway 18R, which is where tower-mode arrivals vacate (121.705, for runways 06/24, is not used: no departure is sent to Ground). Departure 119.055 is the primary Departure/Approach frequency, the same one as the player's Approach frequency (secondary 121.205 not used). The parser takes the unit from its name, so the shared frequency is unambiguous. Wrong frequencies are now flagged.
- **Tower mode, stand-in Approach** (T3, until T4's AI Approach): arrivals are cleared for the ILS on spawn and handed to Tower once on the localizer inside 8 NM, which is when they call. A go-around leaves the Tower frequency (as before: comms back to Approach) and is taken away once it has climbed to the missed-approach altitude. Departures call Tower from the holding point, front of the queue only, 5 s after arriving there; nobody calls Approach.
- **No landing clearance by half a mile** (about 160 ft above the runway on a 3° path): the pilot goes around. ICAO has the pilot go around without a landing clearance; the point is our simplification. A clearance applied before that point counts.
- **Departure queue moves up** at 8 kt when the aircraft in front lines up (70 m slots). Moving up does not occupy the runway. Since 2026-11-18 the slots run along the taxiway behind the S7E holding point: 700 m of OSM centreline (the straightest continuing line at each node from the holding point, traced on `public/tower/ground.json`), hand-set as `QUEUE` in `scripts/build-eham.py` and stored as `entry.queue`; straight back from the holding point without one.
- **Vacate instructions:** a named exit or side that the aircraft can still make at normal braking replaces the pilot's choice; asked for on final, it is kept for the rollout and falls back to the pilot's choice if it can no longer be made. Too late on the rollout → "unable".
- **Hold position / cancel take-off** on the roll is a rejected take-off below rotation speed: it brakes to a stop on the runway, lined up, and waits for a new take-off clearance.
- **A landing clearance takes effect when transmitted** (T3), not when the pilot's readback starts like other instructions: a clearance given just before the go-around point must not lose to the 0.5–1.2 s readback delay or a busy frequency.
- **Tower parser role** (T3): tower phrases are parsed only when the player is Tower, so Approach parsing is unchanged. In tower role "contact departure" means Schiphol Departure (in approach role it still means the radar handoff), and "I say again" is the controller repeating, not a request. "Runway 24" said before a clearance belongs to it (ICAO order is "runway 24, cleared for take-off"). Wind missing from a landing or take-off clearance is an info item (the ICAO example clearances in Doc 4444 ch. 12 and CAP 413 include the surface wind; section not checked).
- **Deferred tower phrases** (T3 → later): "stop" on its own, traffic information ("traffic … on short final"), conditional clearances ("behind the landing A320, line up and wait behind"), "take-off" word rule (ICAO reserves "take-off" for the take-off clearance itself; not checked).
- **Stand-in Approach feed** (T4, replaces the scripted arrivals): arrivals appear on the 18R final 12 NM out at 3000 ft and 180 kt, and are held back until the gap to the previous arrival is at least `feedGap`: the larger of the distance wake minimum and the distance flown at 180 kt during 75 s of runway occupancy plus 0.7 NM, plus the closure while the leader flies its last 5 NM at about its approach speed. Checked by a headless run of a Tower that clears every arrival and says nothing else: no go-arounds in two hours at 40 an hour, for all type mixes, calm and with 25 kt wind. It still hands over at 8 NM (the design said about 6; 8 gives the player more time).
- **Go-arounds come back:** the stand-in Approach takes a go-around once it has climbed to 2000 ft and feeds it onto the final again 4 minutes later, same callsign. It calls Tower again as a new contact.
- **Departures leave the scene 8 NM out** and are scored when handed to Departure (or auto-handed at 2000 ft above the field: a missed handoff in Pro).
- **Runway incursion** (tower, simplified): a cleared arrival that is 0.2 NM from the threshold with another aircraft on the runway goes around, and that counts as an incursion (the controller let it get that close); and a take-off roll that starts with another aircraft on the runway (until the previous departure is past the far end or above 1500 ft). The penalty is big in Pro (−300), none in Beginner, where the PTT bar warns before you send a landing or take-off clearance onto an occupied runway.
- **Departure wake turbulence, time-based between lift-offs:** 2 min for Light or Medium behind Heavy and Light behind Medium; behind an A380, 2 min for Heavy and 3 min for Medium or Light. Doc 4444 departure minima as recalled, not checked against the document. Only in tower mode: the stand-in tower in Approach mode still launches without wake spacing.
- **Late handoffs** (tower, Pro): an arrival that reaches the holding position on its exit without being sent to Ground, or a departure still with Tower at 2000 ft above the field. "When vacated, contact Ground" given on final or on the rollout is accepted; the switch happens when the aircraft turns off. Beginner makes both handoffs automatically.
- **Go-around with the runway free** (tower): a go-around you instruct while nobody is on the runway is noted (−25 in Pro). A go-around you instruct while the runway is occupied costs nothing; only the pilots' own go-arounds (no clearance, runway occupied) count as go-arounds.
- **No airborne separation in tower mode:** conflicts are not detected or scored; spacing on the final is the stand-in Approach's job. Arrival delay isn't scored either; the holding-point wait (ready call to start of the take-off roll) is, at −1 per 10 s in Pro.
- **Tower scoring:** a departure is worth as much as a landing (100). Free play and "Pro · Light" use narrowbodies only; "Pro · Heavies" and "Pro · Peak" use the whole fleet, including the A380 and the PC-12.
- **Tower view detail (enhance.ts):** instanced trees from OSM forest/residential areas (shadows within 3 km only), mown-grass and crop-row textures, tyre marks, buildings on `MeshStandardMaterial` lit by a sky environment map with smooth shading on curved walls. Side-by-side test on an Iris Xe at 1440×900: 0.32 M → 1.09 M triangles, 423–633 → 245–323 fps, same load time. Chosen by the user after the test; no SSAO or bloom.
- **Whisper prompt:** every transcription gets a prompt (Whisper's "previous text") built from the live game: the station, then one example transmission per phrase of the current role, with the callsigns on frequency taken in turn ("KLM 1234, identified. Speedbird 432, turn left heading 210. …"). transformers.js 4.3 has no prompt option, so the worker passes `<|startofprev|>` + prompt tokens as `decoder_input_ids` and cuts them off the output. Synthetic test (espeak-ng, 4 accents, 12 commands, light noise; correct callsign and commands through the parser): tiny 3 → 27 of 48, base 8 → 41 of 48. Cost: about +5% per transcription (Node and Chromium). A bare callsign list scored lower (base 37/48) because Whisper added callsigns nobody said. A transcript that is the start of the prompt (it begins with the station name) is dropped as an echo.
- **Tower desk:** the desk under the window replaces the left strip column in tower mode (design board TowerWest; open question 1 in tower3d.md answered "desk"). The "On runway" bay uses `sim.runwayOccupants`, so the desk and the incursion rules always agree. The ground radar draws every frame from the 3D view's loop (so its view cone follows the camera); the aerodrome map is cached. It shows positions per sim tick, not on a real ASDE's ~1 s sweep, which is a simplification.
- **Tower lighting (simplified from ICAO Annex 14 vol. I ch. 5, from memory, not checked against the document or the Schiphol AIP):** only the runways in use are lit. Edge lights every 60 m (yellow over the last 600 m), centreline every 30 m (red/white alternating 900–300 m before the end, red over the last 300 m; real CAT II/III centrelines are every 15 m), green threshold and red end bars, touchdown-zone barrettes over the first 900 m, a 900 m approach lighting system with a crossbar at 300 m on the landing runway only, green taxiway centreline lights every 30 m along the OSM taxiway lines. No stop bars or runway guard lights yet (apron floodlights: see below). Aircraft landing lights are on for all traffic (all of it is below 10,000 ft). Time of day is a view setting only; it does not change the sim.

- **Visibility and fog (tower):** a scenario may set the prevailing visibility and the starting time of day; without one, the player cycles 10 km+ / 3 km / 1000 m / 400 m from the tower toolbar (fixed in scenarios that set it, so fog can't be switched off in a Pro scenario). The fog is three.js FogExp2 with density 1.978 / V, so contrast falls to 2% at the visibility distance (Koschmieder: (density·V)² = −ln 0.02); 10 km or more keeps the usual clear-day haze. Lights carry further than unlit objects in fog (Allard's law); simplified to 0.4× the scene's fog density for the light points, so at 600 m visibility the runway lights fade out at about 1.2–1.5 km. From 8 km down to 1.5 km the sky and haze blend to an even grey and the sun weakens to 20%. Visibility has no effect on the sim: no low-visibility procedures (Schiphol LVP, CAT II/III holding points, increased spacing on the final, "report runway vacated") because their exact rules weren't checked; the fog scenario has lighter traffic instead. The clock starts at 16:30 UTC for dusk and 21:00 for night (an autumn evening), 14:00 by day. The top bar shows wind, QNH and, in the tower, visibility (amber below 1500 m).

- **Conditional clearances (2026-11-17):** only line-up can be conditional, the form is condition, clearance, condition again ("behind the departing A320, runway 24, line up and wait, behind"), as in ICAO Doc 4444 and CAP 413 conditional-clearance phraseology (recalled, not checked against the documents). The pilot must identify the traffic: a type in the description has to match its family (A320 family by its number, "Boeing 737" for B738/B38M, …; no type matches any), otherwise "unable, traffic not in sight". In our configuration only departing traffic on 24 can be a condition (nothing lands on 24), so "behind the landing …" is always "not in sight". The condition is met when the traffic is rolling at 60 kt or more, or airborne (assumed: it has then passed the S7E entry). Traffic information is a command with no effect, read back "traffic in sight". "Stop" alone is accepted as "stop immediately" with an info note; Doc 4444's form for a roll in progress is "stop immediately" (repeated).
- **Stand-in Approach speed control (2026-11-17):** 160 kt until 4 NM, the usual Schiphol final-approach speed restriction (practice as commonly described for EHAM, not checked against the AIP). Release gap = the larger of wake (distance minimum plus the closure in the last 4 NM) and runway-free (time-based: follower at 0.2 NM no earlier than leader touchdown + 75 s assumed occupancy + 25 s margin, slow leader and fast follower as the worst case). The old distance-based runway term was only safe because 180 kt overstated the follower's speed near the threshold; at 160 kt it let E190 pairs go around (measured occupancy up to 87 s).
- **Tower readback errors (2026-11-17):** runway readback errors swap left and right, or change the number by two; these are the classic mix-ups (L/R, similar-sounding numbers) but the exact set is our choice. The pilot still uses the cleared runway because no other runway is simulated (simplification); an uncaught one counts like any other missed readback error. A misread exit is flown for real (adjacent exit on the same runway).
- **Hold entries (2026-11-17):** right-hand holds only (all published EHAM holds in our data). Sectors from the heading over the fix relative to the outbound heading: 0° to 110° right of it parallel, 0° to 70° left of it teardrop, the rest direct (the ICAO 70° line through the fix, Doc 8168 entry sectors as commonly taught; the ±5° flexibility at sector edges is ignored). Entry legs are one minute, timed once established on the entry heading; no wind correction of the outbound timing (simplification, as for the normal outbound leg).
- **Scenery taxiing, more natural (2026-11-18):** scenery aircraft appear and vanish only outside the camera's horizontal view (plus 10°; buildings are not taken as hiding anything): a departure is placed at a stand out of view when it is planned (any stand when all are in view), and of the parked arrivals over the limit of 8 the oldest out of view goes (up to 16 while all are in view). A departure the sim plans too late to taxi the whole way starts part-way along its route, out of view. A taxiing aircraft holds while another aircraft (scenery or sim, on the ground) is within half of both lengths plus 10 m ahead and 20 m either side of its path; of two that each have the other ahead, the one that started later holds, and nobody holds for more than 120 s per movement, so nothing locks up (they may then pass through each other). Departures are timed to reach the queue 60 s early to absorb the holding, and move up when the queue does.

- **Immediate take-off and expedite vacating (2026-11-18), simplified:** a normal take-off clearance now has 6 s before the roll starts (the crew's last checks; a judgement, so an immediate departure saves something). "Cleared for immediate take-off" rolls at once, and from the holding point lines up rolling at 12 kt instead of stopping. "Line up and wait, be ready for immediate departure" is read back ("ready for immediate departure") but changes nothing until the take-off clearance. "Expedite vacating" (CAP 413 wording) during the approach, the rollout or the vacate: the pilot brakes at 4.5 kt/s instead of the normal rate, switches to an earlier exit it can still make when no exit was named, and taxis off at 30 kt; read back "expediting".
- **Priority arrivals (2026-11-18), simplified:** in free play and the Pro scenarios 8 % of random arrivals ask for priority (never the scripted opening one): half with a medical case ("PAN PAN" three times, station, callsign, "medical emergency on board, request priority landing", then the usual level and ATIS; urgency message order per CAP 413 ch. 8, from memory), half with "minimum fuel" at the end of the check-in (a declaration that no delay can be accepted, not an emergency; ICAO Doc 4444 15.5.4, from memory). The player acknowledges with "roger PAN" / "roger minimum fuel" (pilot: "Roger, callsign"); nothing in the sim changes: the aircraft flies like any other and it is up to the player to take it first. Approach Pro: its delay counts three times (−2 per 10 s on top of the normal −1); a go-around costs another −100 (Beginner −20), in both views. Tower: the stand-in Approach does not reorder the stream; it passes the priority on by landline, and the pilot repeats it when calling Tower ("PAN PAN" ×3 … "medical emergency on board, ILS 18R", or "…, ILS 18R, minimum fuel"). No mayday, no fuel running out, no diversions.
- **Cloud ceiling (2026-11-18), simplified:** one cloud layer per session: a flat, unlit deck at the base height in the tower view (a canvas texture tiled every 2.5 km, the scene haze over it), drawn after the opaque scene so it hides what is above it and not what is below. - **Radar lessons 6–10 and the arrival procedure (2026-11-18), simplified:** the game teaches one standard pattern for 18R: downwind 6 NM beside the final at 3000 ft and about 220 kt, base 12–14 NM out at 2000 ft, a 30° intercept (155 from the west, 210 from the east, rounded to 5° towards the final course) from about 3.5 NM off, joining about 8 NM out, below the glideslope; SUGOL and RIVER traffic on the west side, ARTIP on the east. This is a generic radar-vectoring pattern, not Schiphol's published transitions (which do much the same with fixed routes). The lessons: a whole arrival (check-in to Tower, with a guide path drawn on the scope), joining the localizer (Pro rules: a 90° crossing flies through, then the 30° intercept and the glideslope from below), two in a row (speed and a longer downwind), separation (a level or a 30° turn), departures (climb to FL100, hand to Radar).
